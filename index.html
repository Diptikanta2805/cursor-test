The Bridge Thesis

I study strategy at the intersection of human cognition, technological disruption, and organizational adaptation—bringing a decade of industry analytics experience to rigorous management scholarship.

Before academia, I spent five years as a senior strategist in digital analytics at Accenture and Grant Thornton, where I built data-driven marketing intelligence systems for Fortune 500 clients. That practitioner lens now informs my research on how top management teams navigate generative AI, allocate attention, and orchestrate resources for competitive advantage.

My dissertation agenda examines CEO and TMT cognition, strategic leadership under technological uncertainty, and how firms learn to adopt agentic and generative technologies—questions that sit at the heart of strategic management's next frontier.

CEO & Top Management Team Cognition

How do executives perceive, interpret, and act on environmental signals? I examine cognitive frameworks that shape strategic decision-making under ambiguity—particularly when leaders must evaluate novel, fast-moving technologies whose implications are not yet codified.

Generative AI & Agentic Frameworks

Generative AI is not merely a tool—it reconfigures how firms innovate, partner, and compete. My empirical work analyzes how S&P 500 firms create (or destroy) market value through GenAI adoption, examining the interplay of innovation capabilities, strategic alliances, and implementation choices.

Strategic Leadership & Innovation

Leadership is the mechanism through which strategy becomes action. I investigate how strategic leaders build innovation capabilities, govern alliances, and allocate resources to exploratory versus exploitative activities—especially when navigating platform shifts and autonomous technologies.

Organizational Learning

Firms that learn faster adapt better. I study how organizations absorb lessons from technology implementation, translate analytics insights into strategic action, and build learning routines that sustain competitive advantage across technological generations.

Selected Work

Featured · Job Market Paper

Unlocking Generative AI Adoption: Innovation Capabilities, Alliances, and Implementation Strategy

Diptikanta Panigrahi, Sumanta Singha, Vincent L. Barker

Academy of Management Proceedings, 2025

Despite growing scholarly attention to technological innovation, we lack an understanding of how firms create value from disruptive technologies like generative AI. Analyzing 296 US publicly traded S&P 500 firms during 2023–2024, we find that GenAI adoption generates positive abnormal returns—but these returns diminish for firms with higher innovation capabilities unless complemented by strategic alliances or core-function implementation. Firms with stronger exploratory innovation orientation achieve significantly higher returns when combining strategic partnerships with core function implementation.
