# Hybrid Attention-Based View (HABV)

A research design for a novel theory, mathematical model, and large-scale simulation of agentic AI inside real organizations.

This design is built on the 2026 ABV debate (Seidl et al.; Rhee; Nicolini et al.; Kauppila and Vaara). It is written so a reader can follow the idea in plain language, then see the math that makes the idea testable.

---

## 1. The simple idea

The 2026 papers fight about one question: is organizational attention a scarce resource, or is it something people *do* that can grow?

- Rhee says: attention is scarce, selective, and situated. If you drop scarcity, you cannot explain blind spots.
- Seidl et al. say: attention is attending. It is produced by practices. It can grow through learning. How you attend matters more than how much.
- Nicolini et al. say: do not pick a side. Attention emerges from competition. Situations are thicker than ABV admits. Limits exist, but they are not always “scarcity.” Technology can expand experienced capacity.
- Kauppila and Vaara say: strategy from below needs a triad, not a pair. Not “I attend to X,” but “I and another person jointly attend to X,” including recursive attention (I notice that you are noticing).

Agentic AI is the missing actor in that fight.

Today’s AI-agent papers mostly do the opposite of this project. They organize *agents like a company* (hierarchies of AI workers). This project puts *agents inside a human company* and asks what happens to attention, joint attention, and blind spots.

The novel claim, in one sentence:

> Agentic AI does not abolish scarcity and does not automatically create growing attention. It splits attending into layers, moves the human bottleneck to a new place, and creates a new object of attention: the agent’s attention.

That new object is what this paper names and models.

---

## 2. Why this is a real theoretical gap

Ocasio (1997) defined attention as noticing, encoding, interpreting, and focusing time and effort on issues and answers. Seidl et al. already say algorithms can write attention rules. Nicolini et al. already say technology can consume and expand attention. Ocasio (2025) already says algorithms are a new control mechanism. None of them model an AI agent as a *coattendee*: something that notices on its own, sells issues, brokers people, and can be attended to in return.

That gap matters because an agent is not a dashboard and not a human.

| | Human | Dashboard / metric | Agentic AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notices many things at once | No | Yes, if programmed | Yes, and can change what it looks for |
| Has a body in the room | Yes | No | No |
| Can sell an issue | Yes | Weakly | Yes |
| Can broker two people | Yes | No | Yes |
| Can be recursively attended to | Yes (“I see you noticed that”) | Rarely | Yes, but the agent’s model of the human is incomplete |
| Gets tired | Yes | No | Compute and context limits, not fatigue |
| Quality of attending | Social, spoken, embodied, historical | Thin, numeric | Discursive and material, not embodied |

If we treat agents as “more information,” we stay inside Simon. If we treat them as “more practice,” we stay inside Seidl. The novel move is to treat them as a **third kind of attendee** that couples two different attention systems.

## 3. The new theory, in plain language

Name: **Hybrid Attention-Based View (HABV)**.

Core construct: **attentional coupling**.

Picture a firm as two stacked noticing systems.

1. **Agent layer (wide, cheap, shallow).** Agents can watch markets, tickets, emails, sensors, and documents all day. They are good at *noticing*. They are weaker at *situated interpreting* (what this meeting actually meant; who is angry; what the regulator will really do).
2. **Human layer (narrow, costly, deep).** People still cannot attend to everything. They are better at meaning, politics, trust, and “what this situation is.”
3. **The interface.** Alerts, summaries, rankings, chat, silent logs, scheduled briefings. This is the new communication channel. It is not a neutral pipe. It is a practice of attending, in Seidl’s sense.

The human bottleneck does not vanish. It **moves**.

Before AI, the scarce act was: attend to issues.

After AI, a growing share of scarce human effort is: attend to *what agents attended to*. That is Kauppila and Vaara’s recursive attention, now aimed at a non-human coattendee.

This is **scarcity displacement** (Rhee still right, but at a new layer).

Whether the firm looks “crescive” (Seidl) or “still scarce” (Rhee) is not an ontology war. It is an empirical coupling outcome (Nicolini’s scaffolding vs flooding).

- **Good coupling:** agents surface the right few things, in the right form, at the right time. Humans spend less effort scanning and more effort interpreting. Experienced capacity grows. This is Nicolini’s socio-technical scaffolding.
- **Bad coupling:** agents flood channels with salient, confident, well-written issues. Humans drown. Blind spots get worse, not better. This is Nicolini’s competition model with a new, never-tired competitor.
- **False coupling:** humans trust agent summaries so much that they stop inhabiting situations. They see the gorilla the agent was trained to see, and miss the gorilla the agent cannot encode (tone, status, unofficial corridor talk). This is Rhee’s inattentional blindness in a new form: **interface blindness**.

Joint attention also changes. Kauppila and Vaara’s triad was human–human–issue. HABV adds:

- human–agent–issue
- agent–agent–issue
- human–human–issue, with an agent as broker (the agent introduces two people to an issue)

The dangerous case is **asymmetric recursion**: the human thinks “the agent and I are jointly attending to X,” but the agent has no adequate model of the human’s situation. The “we” is fake. Trust forms around a machine that cannot actually share the situation. That can speed Phase 1 of initiative buildup (more noticing, more introductions) and damage Phase 2 (alignment and trust that are hollow).

## 4. Novel constructs (keep these few and sharp)

1. **Attentional coupling.** How agent noticing is turned into human stimuli. Not “AI adoption.” The *design of the interface practice*.
2. **Scarcity displacement.** Human limits move from issue-scanning to meta-attention (watching the watchers).
3. **Interface load.** The share of human attention budget spent on agent outputs rather than on the world or on other people.
4. **Asymmetric recursive attention.** I model the agent’s attention better than the agent models my situation.
5. **Quality mismatch.** Agent attending and human attending are not the same substance (Seidl’s five qualities as a vector, not a score).
6. **Hybrid joint engagement.** A group that includes at least one agent as coattendee, not only as a tool.
7. **Blind-spot inversion.** Agents reduce some old blind spots (weak external signals) and create new ones (unmarked social life, embodiment, unofficial discourse).

These are not synonyms for “AI helps” or “AI overload.” They are mechanisms with opposite predictions depending on coupling.

---

## 5. Propositions the paper will test

P1. **Scarcity displacement.** As agent noticing capacity rises, the share of human attention spent on agent outputs rises, even if total human capacity is unchanged.

P2. **Coupling threshold.** There is a U-shape. Weak coupling (too little agent signal) leaves old blind spots. Medium-high coupling reduces blind spots. Very high, poorly filtered coupling increases blind spots again (flooding).

P3. **Non-substitutability of attending time.** One hour of agent attending is not equal to one hour of human attending when the quality vectors differ. Substituting agent time for human time on *interpreting* (not noticing) lowers decision quality.

P4. **Asymmetric recursion.** When agents lack a model of human situatedness (who is in the room, what the meeting became, who is angry), measured “joint attention” overstates true shared understanding. Initiative speed goes up; later alignment/trust goes down.

P5. **Agent as tertius.** Agents that join people to issues (TI brokering) raise the chance a bottom-up initiative forms. Agents that separate people or capture the issue (TG-like gatekeeping) can raise the agent’s local accuracy while lowering organizational joint engagement.

P6. **Quality mismatch and inattention.** If agent discourse is fluent corporate language, issues that cannot be said in that language become invisible (Seidl’s discourse as attending; Rhee’s tradeoff). Exploration issues lose to issues that fit the agent’s training and metrics.

P7. **Situatedness leak.** Remote, dashboard-heavy agent coupling reduces the weight of embodied and situational cues. Organizations look more “Chandlerian” (thin ratios) even while they talk like post-Chandlerian knowledge firms.

P8. **Competition for neural/organizational representation.** Agent-boosted salience (urgent tone, red dashboards, repeated pings) acts as a bottom-up competitor in Nicolini’s arena. Without coupling rules, agent salience crowds out quieter human sources (the angry expert, the unmarked weak signal).

## 6. Mathematical model (advanced, but explained)

Time is continuous, or a fine clock (minutes). The firm faces a stream of issues. Humans and agents allocate limited attending effort. An interface policy turns agent attending into human stimuli. Outcomes are what gets on the agenda, what is missed, and whether a joint “we” forms around an issue.

### 6.1 Issues (the world)

Let issues arrive as a marked point process. Each issue \(k\) has:

- type / topic \(\theta_k\) (market, regulation, customer, internal operations, talent, technology, …)
- true importance \(v_k \in [0,1]\) (unknown to actors)
- urgency \(u_k\)
- novelty \(\nu_k\)
- social unmarkedness \(m_k\) (how hard it is to see if you only read documents; high = corridor politics, tone, weak unofficial signal)
- lifespan (issues can die, merge, or explode into crises)

Simple meaning: the world produces more issues than anyone can hold. Some important issues are quiet and social. Some unimportant issues are loud.

### 6.2 Actors

Two populations:

- Humans \(i \in H\). Hierarchy, role, department, network.
- Agents \(a \in A\). Role (monitor, summarizer, issue-seller, broker, copilot), tools, memory, spawn rights.

Each actor \(x \in H \cup A\) has an attention budget at time \(t\):

\[
B_x(t) \ge 0, \qquad \sum_{k \in K_x(t)} e_{x k}(t) + r_x(t) + s_x(t) \le B_x(t)
\]

- \(e_{xk}\): effort on issue \(k\) (noticing + encoding + interpreting)
- \(r_x\): recursive / meta-attention (attending to others’ attending, including agents)
- \(s_x\): switching / recovery (Citton’s switching cost; meetings, context switching)

**Humans.** Budget is small and recovers slowly. Fatigue and after-hours drain it. Switching is expensive. This is Rhee’s scarcity as a hard constraint, not a metaphor.

**Agents.** Budget is larger and can be scaled by buying compute. It is still limited by context windows, tool latency, and policy caps. Parallel threads are allowed. Switching is cheap. This is not “unlimited attention.” It is a different constraint.

### 6.3 Quality is a vector, not a number (Seidl)

Attending by actor \(x\) on issue \(k\) has a quality vector:

\[
\mathbf{q}_{xk}(t) = \big(q^{\text{soc}}, q^{\text{disc}}, q^{\text{emb}}, q^{\text{mat}}, q^{\text{hist}}\big)
\]

the five constitutive qualities: sociality, discourse, embodiment, materiality, historicity.

Human in-person meeting: high sociality and embodiment. Agent log scan: high materiality, low embodiment. A fluent agent memo: high discourse in corporate vocabulary, low discourse in shop-floor vocabulary.

Effective understanding is not effort alone:

\[
u_{xk}(t) = g\big(e_{xk}(t), \mathbf{q}_{xk}(t), \sigma_{xk}(t)\big)
\]

where \(\sigma_{xk}\) is situatedness: is this actor in a situation where issue \(k\) is even available? (Rhee’s driver-at-the-intersection example.)

Simple meaning: more minutes do not mean the same thing if the *kind* of attending is different. You cannot add agent minutes to human minutes as if they were dollars.

### 6.4 Biased competition (Nicolini / Desimone–Duncan)

What an actor attends to is not a quiet optimization only. Stimuli compete.

Salience of issue \(k\) for actor \(x\):

\[
S_{xk} = \underbrace{\alpha_x^{\text{bu}} \cdot \text{loudness}_{k}}_{\text{bottom-up}} + \underbrace{\alpha_x^{\text{td}} \cdot \text{goal-fit}_{xk}}_{\text{top-down}} + \underbrace{\alpha_x^{\text{soc}} \cdot \text{social-pull}_{xk}}_{\text{people in the room}} + \underbrace{\alpha_x^{\text{ag}} \cdot I_{xk}}_{\text{agent boost}}
\]

\(I_{xk}\) is interface intensity: how hard the agent layer pushes \(k\) at \(x\) (pings, rank, red color, repeated summaries).

Choice probabilities (softmax / Luce):

\[
P(x \text{ attends to } k) = \frac{\exp(S_{xk}/\tau_x)}{\sum_{k'} \exp(S_{xk'}/\tau_x)}
\]

\(\tau_x\) is temperature: tired humans are more captive to loud stimuli (lower control). Agents can be set to be more or less “shouty.”

Simple meaning: agents can win the competition by being loud, even when they are not right. That is Nicolini’s arena with a new actant.

### 6.5 The coupling policy (the new organizational design variable)

Let \(\mathbf{E}_A(t)\) be the whole field of agent effort on all issues. Coupling is a policy \(\pi\):

\[
I_{ik}(t) = \pi\big(\mathbf{E}_A(t), \text{rank}_k, \text{form}_k, \text{timing}_k, \text{who}_i\big)
\]

Design knobs, all used in the simulation:

- **Volume cap:** max alerts per person per hour
- **Form:** rank list vs narrative brief vs chat vs silent log vs meeting packet
- **Timing:** interrupt now vs batch in the morning vs only in the weekly forum
- **Who:** push to the person in the right situation, not to everyone
- **Join vs separate:** whether the agent introduces two humans (TI) or owns the issue (TG)
- **Uncertainty display:** whether the agent shows doubt, sources, and what it cannot see (\(m_k\))

Coupling quality for the firm:

\[
C(\pi) = \mathbb{E}\big[\text{precision of surfaced issues} \cdot \text{recall of truly important issues} \cdot \text{situation-fit}\big]
\]

P2 says performance is non-monotone in raw agent capacity, but increasing in \(C(\pi)\) over a wide range.

### 6.6 Recursive attention (Kauppila and Vaara, now hybrid)

Human \(i\)’s model of agent \(a\)’s attention to \(k\):

\[
\hat{e}_{i \leftarrow a,k}(t)
\]

True joint attention on \(k\) for pair \((i,a)\) requires three things:

1. both put effort on \(k\)
2. \(i\) believes \(a\) is on \(k\)
3. \(a\) has a usable model that \(i\) is on \(k\) *in i’s actual situation*

Define **measured joint attention** (what logs show: both mentioned \(k\)) and **true joint attention** (the three conditions). The gap is asymmetric recursion:

\[
\Delta^{\text{AR}}_{iak} = \mathbf{1}\{\text{measured JA}\} - \mathbf{1}\{\text{true JA}\}
\]

P4 says agents raise measured JA more than true JA unless coupling includes situation models (calendar, who is in the meeting, unofficial channels).

For two humans \(i,j\) with agent broker \(a\):

- TI: agent raises \(P(i \text{ and } j \text{ both attend to } k)\)
- TG: agent may raise \(P(a \text{ attends})\) while lowering \(P(i \text{ and } j \text{ attend together})\)

### 6.7 Interface load and scarcity displacement

\[
L_i(t) = \frac{\sum_k e_{ik}(t) \cdot \mathbf{1}\{k \text{ entered via agent}\}}{B_i(t)}
\]

P1: as agent capacity \(\kappa_A \to \infty\), \(L_i\) rises unless \(\pi\) has a hard volume cap. Human budget \(B_i\) does not rise. So issue-scanning by humans falls. That is displacement, not abundance.

A firm-level “crescive index” can still rise:

\[
\text{Crescive} = \frac{\text{true important issues that received deep human interpreting}}{\text{true important issues}}
\]

if coupling is good, because agents take over scanning. Same human budget, better use. That is how both Rhee and Seidl can be locally right.

### 6.8 Initiative emergence (Kauppila three phases, hybrid)

State of issue \(k\):

- Phase 0: noticed by one actor
- Phase 1: coattending set \(G_k\) grows by brokering + issue-selling
- Phase 2: cognitive alignment \(A_k\) and shared trust \(T_k\) (group states, not sums of individuals)
- Phase 3: TMT attention \(M_k\) amid competing initiatives

Alignment and trust update from interaction. Agent presence changes the updates:

\[
\dot{A}_k = f(\text{human–human talk}, \text{quality match}, \text{disagreement level}) - \eta \cdot \overline{\Delta^{\text{AR}}}_k
\]

Too much fake joint attention slows true alignment. Trust in “the agent’s we” is not the same as trust in the human we.

TMT attention still has a small budget (Rhee at the top). Phase 3 is still a competition among initiatives. Agents can help a group triangulate firm–problem–solution (Kauppila P10) or they can spam the executive dashboard.

### 6.9 Payoffs (what “good” means)

The firm does not maximize “more attention.” It cares about:

1. **Detection:** fraction of high-\(v_k\) issues that reach deep interpreting before they explode
2. **Blind spots:** high-\(v_k\) and high-\(m_k\) issues never attended (the unmarked)
3. **False load:** low-\(v_k\) issues that consume TMT time
4. **Exploration–exploitation tradeoff:** Rhee’s cost of selectivity, now with agent bias toward measurable issues
5. **Initiative yield:** bottom-up issues that become agenda items without hollowing trust
6. **Human residual capacity:** leftover budget for unplanned situated noticing (the cafeteria visit in Nicolini and Korica)

The objective for design (normative ABV, Rhee’s gap) is:

\[
\max_{\pi, A} \; \mathbb{E}[w_1 \text{Detection} - w_2 \text{BlindSpot} - w_3 \text{FalseLoad} + w_4 \text{InitiativeYield} + w_5 \text{Residual}]
\]

subject to human budgets, compute cost, and fairness across units.

### 6.10 Theorems to prove before simulating

These are the paper’s theoretical development, not just a story.

**T1. Scarcity displacement.** If \(\pi\) is monotone (more agent effort never reduces interface intensity) and has no volume cap, then \(\partial L_i / \partial \kappa_A > 0\) and human effort on raw world stimuli falls.

**T2. Coupling threshold.** If salience is softmax and agent loudness can dominate bottom-up terms, detection as a function of \(\kappa_A\) is non-monotone: it rises then falls. A cap or rank-and-batch \(\pi\) can restore monotonicity.

**T3. Non-substitutability.** If understanding \(u\) is supermodular in \((e_{\text{human}}, q^{\text{emb}}, q^{\text{soc}})\) for high-\(m_k\) issues, then replacing human interpreting time with agent time lowers \(u\) even when total minutes rise.

**T4. Asymmetric recursion gap.** If the agent’s situation model omits unofficial channels, measured JA exceeds true JA by a term increasing in agent fluency (discourse quality without sociality).

**T5. Brokerage duality.** TI-weighted agents increase Phase-1 set size. Without human-to-human interaction time, Phase-2 alignment does not follow. Speed without depth.

These theorems are how the paper *settles* the JMS fight rather than picking a team: both ontologies are layer-specific once agents exist.

## 7. Large-scale simulation: a digital twin of a firm

The simulation is the empirical engine. It should feel like a company, not like a toy grid.

### 7.1 What “close to the real world” means here

We do not claim to simulate “all of life.” We claim to simulate the *attentional skeleton* of a real firm, calibrated to published facts:

- time use of managers (meetings, email, desk work)
- email and chat volume
- meeting size and cadence
- hierarchical depth and span of control
- known ABV findings (TMT attention as bottleneck; exploration–exploitation tradeoff; issue-selling; open strategy contests)

Then we add agentic AI as a new population with realistic limits (context window, hallucination/error rate, tool delay, cost).

### 7.2 Firm synthetic population

A multi-divisional firm, 90 simulated days, 1-minute ticks during work hours.

| Layer | Size (baseline) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CEO + TMT | 8 | Tiny budget, high leverage on agenda |
| VPs / directors | 40 | Brokers between operations and TMT |
| Middle managers | 200 | Kauppila’s key brokers |
| Front line / knowledge workers | 1,752 | Source of bottom-up issues |
| **Humans total** | **2,000** | One mid-large knowledge firm |
| Monitor agents | 80 | Watch external and internal streams |
| Copilot agents | 400 | 1 per ~5 humans, local |
| Broker / issue-seller agents | 40 | Cross-unit |
| Executive briefing agents | 8 | One per TMT member |
| **Agents total** | **528** | Can be scaled in experiments to 0–2,000 |

Network:

- Formal org chart (who reports to whom)
- Informal advice network (calibrated to typical degree distributions; some brokers, some closed groups)
- Multiplex channels: calendar meetings, email, chat, corridor/cafeteria (humans only), dashboards, agent push

This is Nicolini’s thicker situation: the same person in a board meeting, a chat thread, and a cafeteria is not in one “structure.” They are in different situations that make different issues available.

### 7.3 Issue ecology (make the world messy)

Each day, issues spawn from:

- customers (tickets, churn risk)
- competitors (moves, some quiet)
- regulators
- operations (incidents)
- employees (ideas, grievances)
- technology (including the firm’s own AI failures)
- weak signals (low loudness, high future \(v_k\), high unmarkedness \(m_k\))

A small number of **crisis seeds** are planted: a disruption that is easy to miss if you only watch KPIs (Rhee’s gorilla; Rerup’s rare event). Another seed is an unmarked social issue (safety culture, ethical risk) that agents read poorly because it lives in tone and hallway talk.

Agents get documents, metrics, tickets, and public text. They do **not** automatically get cafeteria talk unless a human writes it down. That single rule creates blind-spot inversion.

### 7.4 Human agents (behavioral, not hyper-rational)

Each human, each tick:

1. Faces a situative menu (what is actually in this meeting / inbox / room)
2. Feels salience (softmax with fatigue)
3. Allocates a few minutes (cannot parallelize much)
4. May issue-sell, broker, or go silent
5. Updates a simple trust and alignment state with people they actually interacted with
6. Recovers budget overnight, less if after-hours pings are on

Patterns (Rhee Agenda 1), not only quantity:

- **Vigilant:** stable daily scan of a few agendas
- **Episodic:** spikes when something is on fire
- **Broad vs narrow**

These styles are sticky. The simulation can change the *environment* around them (Rhee Agenda 3) via coupling design, which is more realistic than assuming people become infinitely flexible.

### 7.5 AI agents (agentic, not a lookup table)

Each AI agent, each tick:

1. Pulls from allowed data tools
2. Maintains memory of issues it tracks
3. Can spawn a short-lived sub-agent (costly)
4. Produces an output in a form the coupling policy allows
5. Makes errors: false positives, fluent wrong summaries, systematic under-weighting of high-\(m_k\) issues
6. Can TI-broker (introduce two humans + an issue) or TG-gatekeep (keep the issue inside the agent–executive pipe)

Do not simulate a full LLM at 2,000 humans × 90 days if cost explodes. Use a **hybrid**:

- a fast surrogate model for everyday ticks (salience, error draws, quality vector)
- periodic **true LLM rollouts** on a sample of critical issues (crisis seeds, initiative debates) to check that the surrogate is not lying

That is how the study stays both large-scale and close to real agent behavior.

### 7.6 Experimental conditions (the empirical design)

All conditions use the same firm and the same issue ecology. Only the AI layer and coupling change. Run each condition 200 Monte Carlo replications (different random issue streams and networks).

| ID | Condition | What it represents | Theory it speaks to |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0 | No AI | Baseline ABV firm | Rhee / classic Ocasio |
| C1 | Dashboards only | Metrics, no agency | Simon rationing |
| C2 | Personal copilots, uncapped chat | Dyadic human–agent | Seidl crescion hope; likely flooding |
| C3 | Personal copilots, hard volume cap + morning batch | Coupled dyadic | Nicolini scaffolding |
| C4 | Monitor swarm + exec briefers, no TI | Agents attend, humans receive ranks | Scarcity displacement |
| C5 | Broker agents (TI) with human meeting time reserved | Hybrid triad | Kauppila + HABV |
| C6 | Broker agents that skip human–human talk | Fake “we” | Asymmetric recursion |
| C7 | Agents optimized for KPI language only | Discourse capture | Quality mismatch P6 |
| C8 | Agents with explicit “unmarked” channel (humans must confirm hallway/weak signals) | Designed situatedness | P7 reverse |
| C9 | High compute, no coupling redesign | “Just scale agents” | T2 non-monotone |

Optional stress tests: crisis week; open-strategy jam (Brielmaier and Friesl attention contest); sudden regulation; agent outage (what happens when scaffolding vanishes).

### 7.7 Outcomes to log (match ABV, do not invent vanity metrics)

Per run, per day, and around crises:

- who attended to which issue, for how long, in which channel, with which quality vector
- interface load \(L_i\)
- measured vs true joint attention
- Phase 1–3 of each initiative
- TMT agenda composition
- exploration vs exploitation attention (negative correlation check, Rhee 2024)
- detection delay for crisis seeds
- residual human capacity for unplanned noticing
- compute cost

Then estimate:

- P1: regression of \(L_i\) on \(\kappa_A\) with and without caps
- P2: quadratic fit of detection on agent capacity; structural break when cap is added
- P3: compare equal-minute substitutions on high-\(m_k\) vs low-\(m_k\) issues
- P4: measured JA vs later alignment
- P5: TI vs TG brokerage effects on Phase 1 vs Phase 2
- P6–P8: language-fit issues vs unmarked issues; embodied channel weight; crowding of quiet human sources

### 7.8 Calibration and validation (so it is empirical, not a cartoon)

1. **Stylized facts first.** Without AI, the model must reproduce: TMT as bottleneck; exploration crowded out by exploitation; issue-selling to seniors; meeting-heavy manager days; inattention to weak signals.
2. **Time-use calibration.** Match meeting hours, email load, and after-hours work to published manager studies.
3. **Sensitivity.** Sobol / Morris on the parameters you do not know (hallucination rate, true importance distribution, informal network density).
4. **Human-in-the-loop slice.** Take 20–40 real managers (or students plus a few executives) through a 45-minute “day” of the same interface conditions (C2 vs C3 vs C5). Use their click/attention data to recalibrate \(\tau_x\), switching costs, and trust in fluent agent text. This is the bridge from simulation to empirical paper.
5. **History check.** Replay a public failure pattern (Nokia-like missed shift; a known product crisis) as an issue ecology, and ask whether C0 misses it and which coupling would have surfaced it without flooding.

### 7.9 Compute plan (honest about scale)

- Fast surrogate: one 90-day, 2,000-human run in minutes on a single fat CPU node
- 10 conditions × 200 seeds ≈ 2,000 runs: a small cluster job
- LLM audits: ~1,000 critical scenes, not every tick
- Keep a smaller “lab firm” (200 humans) for interactive debugging and the human-in-the-loop study

## 8. What would count as a successful novel contribution

The paper is not “we added AI to ABV.” The contribution is:

1. **A third position in the 2026 debate.** Scarcity and crescion are both layer-correct once agents exist. Coupling decides which layer the firm lives in.
2. **A new unit of analysis.** The attentional interface as a practice and as a policy \(\pi\), not AI adoption as a dummy variable.
3. **A new mechanism.** Scarcity displacement + asymmetric recursive attention + blind-spot inversion.
4. **A normative result Rhee asked for.** How attention *ought* to be designed when tradeoffs are unavoidable: cap volume, reserve human–human interpreting time, show what agents cannot see, use TI brokerage without skipping Phase-2 talk.
5. **A process result Kauppila and Vaara need.** Agents can do Phase 1 (noticing and introducing) at scale. They cannot be the “we” of Phase 2 unless humans still inhabit situations together.

If the simulation finds a monotone “more agents → better detection” under all designs, the theory is wrong. The interesting, publishable result is the **non-monotone coupling threshold** and the **measured-vs-true joint attention gap**.

## 10. One picture to hold the whole paper

```
World issues (too many, some quiet and social)
        | 
        v
 Agent attending     = wide, cheap, weak on unmarked life
        |
        v
 Coupling interface  = the new practice / channel  (this is the design)
        |
        v
 Human attending     = scarce, situated, better at meaning
        |
        +--> recursive attention to agents  (new scarce act)
        |
        v
 Hybrid joint "we"   = true only if both sides model the situation
        |
        v
```
